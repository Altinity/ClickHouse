## 1. THE FIX — no design yet, no task yet

**[F] Authoritative per-namespace chain + complete-cut gate.** The committed-before-release direction for
`{#list-as-journal-dataloss-2026-07-25}`. Core requirement: **GC must not advance a cursor over records
whose enumeration it cannot prove complete** — completeness must become provable from the data rather than
assumed from a listing.

Requirements the evidence adds:

- Do NOT assume holes are page-shaped. They are short adjacent runs (2–15 keys), not ~1000-key pages. A fix
  keyed on page boundaries would miss them.
- Do NOT rely on the store. RustFS demonstrably answers inconsistently; AWS S3 is untested. The fix must
  hold against a store that lies.
- KEEP probe A. It is why no damage occurred and it is the only field detector for this class.
- KEEP the HEAD verdict. It separates two opposite defects and is unrecoverable after the fact.
- Close probe A's two blind spots via the chain, NOT by widening its witness rule — that was considered and
  rejected as a permanent-cursor-block risk.

## 3. Product fixes

### 3.1 Performance

- **[F] Round-scoped manifest body cache** (#22). Measured lever: 39.6% of intake manifest fetches are
  re-reads. 7,565 edges over 4,573 distinct manifests. **Intra-transaction redundancy is exactly ZERO** —
  all of it is cross-transaction: 2,991 manifests carry both an add and a remove edge in one window, so the
  body is fetched once on publish and again on drop. Each avoided edge saves TWO round trips (HEAD + GET).
  An op- or transaction-scoped cache would save nothing. Needs a memory bound; manifests are not small.
  Do NOT touch the HEAD-before-GET pair — protocol step, standing veto.
- **[F] `pending_deletes` cost.** 77.2 SECONDS in a single occurrence, against 243 ms for the next worst
  phase — three orders of magnitude, on a 20-minute run. Never examined; the perf work has been looking at
  `fold_ref_intake` because that dominated the 4-hour data.
- **[F] fsck cannot run on a real pool** (#21). Entry gate timed out on a **5.5 GB** pool; an earlier
  reading showed `reachable=0` after 160 s, i.e. the scan had not finished its first phase. #13 made the
  failure honest (no more false "PERSISTENT dangling"), it did NOT buy the check any time. Options: scale
  the budget with pool size; give the entry gate `--partial` deliberately and treat it as a lower bound
  (sound HERE because a partial scan finding `dangling > 0` still fails correctly — the opposite of the
  waiter case, where proof of absence is the whole point); or make fsck cheaper.

### 3.2 Correctness

- **[F] Probe A's two blind spots.** (a) a hole reproducing IDENTICALLY in both enumerations; (b) a
  namespace dropped WHOLESALE from one enumeration — no `ref_tables` entry, so the comparison loop never
  visits it. Should fall out of the chain (§1) rather than be patched into the probe.
  **MOOT (Task T5): probe A is deleted outright, not patched — there is no detector left to have blind
  spots.**
- **[F] The residual `eraseView` post-commit window.** Can still throw AFTER the durable commit.
- **[F] Walk 1 / walk 2 strictness asymmetry.** Walk 1 skips an unparseable ref key SILENTLY; walk 2 throws
  `CORRUPTED_DATA` and aborts the round. Produces an abort rather than a hole, so not a defect — but two
  code paths disagreeing about what is fatal is worth tidying.
